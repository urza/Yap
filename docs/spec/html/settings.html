Settings

Settings is the one page where a person shapes their own Yap: profile, look, ways back into the account, devices, notifications, time format and their GIF library. It opens from the status menu and from a few links in the app. Every change saves by itself; there is no Save button anywhere on the page.

Anatomy

  1. Frame. The same header and sidebar as a conversation. The title area shows a back arrow and "Settings".
  2. Page heading. "Settings" with a chip on the right that reads "Saving..." then "Saved" for profile changes, and the line "Changes are saved automatically."
  3. Cards. One column of cards, at most 600 px wide, in this order: Profile, Appearance, Text Size, Login Link, Secret Code, Active Sessions, Install as App, Device Push Notifications, Allow / Mute Notifications, Time & Date, My GIFs, and a collapsed DEBUG INFO card at the end.
  4. Notice. After "Sign out all other devices", an amber notice above the heading with a close control.

Rules

Page

ST-01Settings opens from "Settings" and "Notification settings" in the status menu and from "Manage your GIFs in Settings" in the GIF picker; the bot's messages point to it by name. "Notification settings" and the picker link scroll to their sections. A signed-out visit goes to the login page. Settings needs a connection.

ST-02Every control saves at once on change. Profile text saves after a short pause in typing and when the user leaves the page, so nothing typed is lost. A failed save shows "Could not save. Try again." next to the control.

ST-03The sidebar and the header on Settings show live state, as on a conversation.

Profile

ST-04The avatar shows at 80 px with "Upload Picture" or "Change Picture" and, when a picture exists, "Remove". A picture is any image up to 10 MB; the server crops it to a square. A rejected file says "Choose an image up to 10 MB". Remove needs no confirmation.

ST-05Display Name holds up to 64 characters, with the username as its placeholder and the hint that it can contain symbols and emoji. Empty means the username is shown.

ST-06Bio holds up to 150 characters with a live counter. Location holds up to 100 characters, with "Detect" when the server can tell the country from the address.

ST-07A profile change shows at once in the user's own header and on their messages, and on other people's screens at once.

Appearance and text size

ST-08Appearance shows a grid of theme cards, each with a preview, an accent dot and the name; the chosen one has a check badge. The themes are Discord Dark (the default), Midnight, Nord, Ocean, Sunset, Aurora, Terminal, Neon Glow, Tea House, Daylight and Solarized Light. A tap repaints the whole app at once and saves. A theme that no longer exists falls back to the default.

ST-09Tea House changes its picture with the device's time of day, every two hours, on its own.

ST-10Text Size has six presets, 14, 15, 16 (Default), 17, 18 and 20 px, each shown as "Aa" at its size. A tap rescales all text at once and saves. The theme and the text size follow the account to other devices on their next load.

Ways back in

ST-11Login Link opens with one line that sums up the ways back into the account: "This account is tied to this device only.", or the ways the person has, in the order login link, secret code, smart login.

ST-12With a link: the hint, the URL with its code masked, Reveal and Copy, then "New Link", "Revoke" and the expiry choice. Without a link: the hint and "Create Login Link". The rules are on the sign-in page.

ST-13"Allow smart login" is a switch under the link, shown only while the admin allows smart login. It is on by default.

ST-14Secret Code sits below Login Link. Why: the link is the preferred way back; the code is the fallback. With a code: the masked code, Reveal and Copy, "New Code" and "Disable". Without: the hint and "Enable Secret Code". Status lines show for 3 seconds.

Devices

ST-15Active Sessions lists the account's connected devices with a phone or desktop icon, "This device" on the current one, the network address and "Active {time}". The current device is always listed. "Sign out all other devices" is always available, since a device can hold a sign-in without being connected, and acts without a confirmation; the page then shows the notice "All other devices have been signed out." with the new secret code when one exists.

ST-16Install as App shows "Install Yap" with its explanation, or "Yap is installed as an app on this device." inside the installed app. See Offline and installation.

Notifications

ST-17Device Push Notifications shows the state of this device, the browser permission, the count of subscribed devices, and the device list with "Remove" on each. One action fits the state: enable on this device, turn off on this device, or the reason why neither is possible. "Send a test to all devices" shows when there are devices.

ST-18Allow / Mute Notifications has the server-wide "Allow" or "Mute" with a duration, then the "Direct messages" and "Rooms" rows with "Allow all", "Individual" and "Mute all", each Individual mode listing its conversations with a bell. The rules are on the notifications page. The mute hint reads "Muted for another {time}." with a proper singular and counts down while the page is open.

Time & Date

ST-19"Your time" shows the current time in the user's time zone with "−", "+" and "Detect". The zone was detected at sign-up and stays until changed here. "−" and "+" shift the shown time by one hour from where it is, within 14 hours of universal time either way; after an adjustment the zone is a fixed offset and daylight saving no longer applies. "Detect" takes the browser's zone again. The language detected at sign-up decides the defaults below and has no control of its own.

ST-20Clock offers 24-hour and 12-hour; Date order offers day-month-year, month-day-year and year-month-day; Separators appears when that language and the zone's country format differently. Each is a row of cards that show the current time or today's date in that form, the one matching the language first, and each works with the keyboard. The choices apply to every time in the app.

My GIFs

ST-21My GIFs is the user's pool: everything starred plus their own uploads, in folders. The toolbar has folder chips ("All", "Unsorted", one per folder), a filter field from nine GIFs on, "＋ Add GIFs", "Import zip" and "Export". A quota bar shows "{used} of {quota} used" for the user's own uploads; starred GIFs are free.

ST-22Add takes several .gif, .webp or short video files at once, up to 50 MB each, into the open folder, with a progress line and one error line per refused file. Import takes one zip up to the configured size (1 GB by default) with at most 1000 files of 50 MB each, whose subfolders become folders and whose tag list, when present, adds tags. Export downloads the pool as a zip that imports back without loss, except starred provider GIFs the server never stored.

ST-23Cards show newest first and refresh at once when a star or an upload changes the pool elsewhere. A card shows the preview, up to four tag chips and the folder choice. Tags are edited in place, lowercased, 2 to 64 characters each, and belong to the GIF, so a change affects search for everyone who can see it. A new folder is created from the folder choice, named with up to 64 characters; a folder disappears when its last GIF leaves it.

ST-24"Delete" on an own upload asks "Sure?" before it deletes the file; a file still used in chat or by others is removed from the pool instead and keeps counting against the quota while it exists. "✕" unstars at once. Six cards show first, then 24 more per tap.

Debug

ST-25DEBUG INFO is a collapsed card with what the browser keeps for Yap on this device: cached files by group and their size, the storage estimate, and "Clear this cache" for the media cache. Clearing keeps drafts and unsent messages.

Phones

ST-26At 600 px or less the avatar sits above its buttons, the notification rows stack with full-width controls and 44 px buttons, the link controls wrap, and the GIF grid has two columns.
